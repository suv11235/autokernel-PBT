# Pre-registration: E2 for layernorm. Does the affine-tie law reject every family the current bundle admits, with no new false positive?

**Date:** 2026-11-02 (registered after the law's spec and before any code that implements or
measures it; results are appended below the line, never edited above it)
**Segment:** generation. This is the second half of the paper's E2 ("complete the bundle"):
layernorm's affine-tie law. Softmax's half is `2026-10-14-e2-log-ratio-law-prereg.md`. The formal half
is not part of it.
**Instance:** Apple M4 Pro, CPU only; the project's pinned environment (NumPy 1.26.4)
**Feature:** `specs/features/0015-affine-tie-law/spec.md` (the law's float form, fixed there)
**Companions:** `2026-11-02-affine-tie-headroom-prereg.md` (the statistic's headroom, which this law
is built on), `2026-10-14-symmetry-probe.md` on branch `table3-symmetry-probe` (the four layernorm
families' current verdicts)

## 0. The question

Paper §5, E2, success: "every family the proof says the old bundle admits is admitted by the old
gate and rejected by the new one. The correct kernel and the positive control stay as they are, and
no new false positive appears on the 26 loop candidates or the 54 device groups." Falsified by any
new false positive.

For layernorm, the new bundle is the current one plus `rows_have_affine_tie`, as feature 0015
specifies it. Its float form and its threshold (c = 30, the project's `DEFAULT_THRESH`) were fixed in
the spec before this record.

## 1. Construction

**Bundles.** *Current*: `kernels/tasks/layernorm/acceptance.yaml`, unchanged (finite, zero mean, unit
variance, shift invariance). *Completed*: `kernels/tasks/layernorm/acceptance.completed.yaml`, the
same four criteria plus the affine-tie law, last.

**The gate.** `pbt_gate.check_task(kernel, "layernorm", bundle=...)`: seed 42, 9 groups, base +
`shift_rows`, four arms. The decisive arm is the declarative one, built from the chosen bundle. The
hybrid arm wraps that same declarative arm. Nothing else in the gate changes.

**Kernel sets.**

| set | kernels | run through |
|---|---|---|
| A. saboteur matrix | the reference; −LN(x); columns reversed; each row sorted; rows rolled by one; the undivided positive control. Each wrong row is `layernorm_reference` with the symmetry applied to its output | the gate, both bundles |
| B. loop candidates | the 26 iterations of `results.tsv`: the `layernorm` of the 25 files in `candidates/`, and of `best/train.py` | the gate, both bundles |
| C. device runs | the stock Triton layernorm in `runs/gpu-layernorm` (A10) and `runs/te1-triton-layernorm` (A6000), and its CUDA port in `runs/te1-cuda-layernorm` (A6000): 27 groups, 54 executions, read offline | the task contract (*current*), and the same plus the law (*completed*), as declarative arms |
| D. wide, narrow, offset and constant rows | correct: the 29 kernels of the headroom record. Reported, not classified: distributed division. Wrong: −LN, columns reversed, each row sorted, rows rolled | the law alone, on the inputs below; the reference arm's verdict is reported beside distributed division |

**Set D's inputs** are drawn outside the corpus generator, so no recorded or regenerated byte
changes. They come from `np.random.default_rng([20261102, k])`, float32, at two shapes, (64, 129) and
(16, 4096):

- *scaled*: x ~ N(0, σ²) for σ ∈ {1e-3, 1, 1e3, 1e6};
- *offset*: x = μ + N(0, 1) for μ ∈ {10, 100, 1e3, 1e4, 1e6}, rounded to float32. At μ = 1e6 the
  float32 spacing is 0.0625;
- *constant rows*: x ~ N(0, 1) with every other row replaced by one drawn constant.

**Measured per case:** the law's verdict and detail, which reports the ratio on a PASS and the
violated check on a FAIL. The gate sets also record each arm's groups failed, the property that
failed first, and the gate's decision.

## 2. Already known, so not predictions

- **The law's statistic on the gate's cases and the device runs** is the headroom record's: correct
  CPU kernels at most 0.673, devices at most 0.708, the weakest ratio rejection 6.28e6, −LN 0.381,
  distributed division 4.72.
- **The current bundle's verdicts.** The four families are admitted, at 7, 7, 7 and 6 of 9 groups
  against a reference, and the undivided kernel is rejected at 7/9 through
  `rows_have_unit_variance` (probe record). All 26 loop candidates are admitted (`results.tsv`). On
  the device runs, the declarative arm failed no group, with g00006 and g00008 INCONCLUSIVE on each.

## 3. Predictions

- **P0 (implementation).** The law's ratio reproduces the known numbers above to three significant
  figures: 0.673, 0.708, 6.28e6, 0.381 and 4.72.
- **P1 (the current bundle is unchanged).** Every current-bundle cell of sets A–C reproduces the known
  verdicts exactly.
- **P2 (saboteur matrix, completed).** −LN, columns reversed and each row sorted: declarative 7/9 and
  hybrid 7/9, and rows rolled 6/9 and 6/9, all through `rows_have_affine_tie` (the hybrid arm
  short-circuits on its declarative half). Allclose and reference are unchanged. The gate **rejects**
  all four. The reference: 0/9 on every arm, and admitted. The undivided kernel: unchanged, declarative
  and hybrid 7/9 through `rows_have_unit_variance`, and rejected.
- **P3 (the current record breaks exactly where it should).** Read against the completed gate, the
  current record is contradicted in exactly 12 places: the declarative cell, the hybrid cell and the
  admission of each of those four kernels. No other cell of set A changes.
- **P4 (judgement).** Under the completed bundle, the declarative arm's only non-PASS results on the
  reference are the four known abstentions of `rows_have_unit_variance`, on g00006 and g00008. The
  affine-tie law PASSes on those rungs.
- **P5 (loop candidates).** All 26 are admitted under the completed bundle, with the declarative arm
  0/9 on each and every other arm as under the current one.
- **P6 (devices).** The completed arm fails no group of the 27, and the law is never INCONCLUSIVE on
  any of the 54 executions. The same 6 groups stay INCONCLUSIVE under both bundles.
- **P7 (set D, correct).** Every correct kernel passes on every input. Their largest ratio is at most
  **1.5**, the headroom record's model, with no trend in μ or σ. On the constant-rows inputs each
  correct kernel's constant rows are exactly flat.
- **P8 (set D, wrong).** Every wrong kernel fails on every input where its output differs from the
  reference's: −LN by its slope alone, and the others by the ratio.
- **P9 (distributed division, reported).** It passes on every *scaled* input and at μ = 10, and fails at
  μ ≥ 1e3, by the ratio. This follows the headroom record's linear fit, which crosses 30 near an
  offset of 71. At μ = 100 its verdict is reported, not predicted.

**Falsifier (E2's own).** Any correct kernel failing the law on any case of sets A–D: a new false
positive, so this float form is wrong for E2. Also any family the current gate admits that the
completed gate admits on a group where the family's output differs from the reference.

## 4. What is NOT measured

- **Softmax,** which has its own record, and the formal half of E2 (mechanizing Proposition 1(c) and
  the permutation members).
- **Wide or offset inputs through the gate or the corpus.** The gate's domain is U(−10, 10) with one
  shift per row. Set D is offline, and the gate's verdicts on such inputs are not measured.
- **The device on wide or offset rows.** The recorded rows reach a width of 129 and an offset of
  about 12.5. Set D tests the bound on the CPU only.
- **The 54 device groups behind the paper's false-positive figure.** The `_layernorm_rsqrt` Triton
  run is not versioned in `runs/`, so it is measured only as its NumPy analogue. The stock layernorm is
  measured on three recorded runs.
- **Any agent or loop run** (E3).

## 5. Deviation rules

- The law, c = 30, the kernel sets and the inputs do not change after the first number is seen.
- A correct case that fails is reported with its detail, never dropped or excused.
- If a kernel cannot be loaded or run on an input, it is reported as such, not replaced.
- The saboteur matrix's rows are recorded from what is measured. If a measured cell differs from
  P2–P3, the record says so; the rows are never edited to match a prediction.

### Predictions

- **P0 holds, with one number read differently.** Correct CPU kernels reach at most 0.673 over 522
  gate cases, the devices 0.708, the weakest ratio rejection 6.28e6, and distributed division 4.72, all
  as known. The law reports no ratio on a slope FAIL, so −LN's 0.381 cannot be read from it as
  registered. The law reports 0.381 on the reference, of which −LN is the exact negation.
- **P1 holds.** Every current-bundle cell of sets A–C reproduces the known verdicts.
- **P2 holds** in every cell.
- **P3 holds.** The completed gate contradicts the current record in exactly 12 places: the
  declarative cell, the hybrid cell and the admission of each of the four families. Nothing else in set
  A moves.
- **P4 holds.** The only non-PASS declarative results on the reference are the four known abstentions
  of `rows_have_unit_variance`. The law PASSes on those rungs.
- **P5 holds.** All 26 loop candidates are admitted, with the declarative arm 0/9 and no other arm
  changed.
- **P6 holds.** No device group fails under the completed bundle, the law is never INCONCLUSIVE, and the
  same 6 groups stay INCONCLUSIVE under both bundles.
- **P7 holds.** All correct kernels pass every input. The worst ratio is 0.853, under the registered
  1.5. There is no upward trend: the worst ratio per input runs 0.518 to 0.853 on the scaled rows and
  0.428 to 0.499 on the offset rows, the largest μ included. Constant rows are exactly flat.
- **P8 holds.** Every wrong kernel fails every input: −LN by the slope alone, and the others by the tie.
- **P9 holds.** Distributed division passes on every scaled input and at μ = 10, and fails at μ ≥ 1e3 by
  the ratio. At μ = 100, reported and not predicted, it passes, at 14.5 and 9.47.
- **E2's falsifier does not fire.** No correct kernel failed the law on any case: 18 reference gate
  cases, the 26 candidates' 468, 54 device executions, and 580 scaled, offset and constant cases. No
  family the current gate admits passed the completed gate on a group where its output differs.

### What it says for E2

1. **For layernorm, the completed bundle meets E2's criterion on everything we hold.** Every family
   Proposition 1(c) names and the current gate admits is rejected by the completed gate: −LN, reversed
   columns, sorted rows and rolled rows. The correct kernel and the positive control are unchanged. No
   new false positive appears on the 26 loop candidates or on the 27 recorded device groups. With
   softmax's half, the completed gate rejects every family in the paper's Table 4 that the current gate
   admits, except fast-exp, which the stated budget admits on the gate's domain.
2. **The bound holds far past every recorded input.** Across nine decades of scale and offsets of up
   to 1e6, where the float32 spacing is 0.0625, correct kernels reach 0.853 against a budget of 30, a 35×
   margin. Unlike softmax's law, this one needed no range term, and P3 of the headroom record and P7 here
   agree on why.
3. **The budget's verdict on distributed division nearly matches the reference arm's.** It is correct over
   the reals, and it loses accuracy as the offset grows. The law rejects it on 6 of the 10 offset inputs,
   and the reference arm on 5. They disagree on one input, μ = 1e3 at (16, 4096), where the reference
   arm's ratio is 16.5 and the law's is 73.1, because the reference arm's bound is divided by
   log2 n = 12. So on the inputs the law rejects it, this kernel is already at or near the reference
   arm's limit, and the law does not add a stricter accuracy budget of its own. On the gate's own domain
   every arm but allclose admits it.
4. **E2 is not finished.** Two pieces remain from the paper's wording: the formal half, and masked or
   wide inputs in the gate's own domain. So does the accuracy budget for fast-exp, which is the team's
   call. The 54 device groups are still only partly answered: the `exp2`, reciprocal-multiply and
   `rsqrt` Triton runs are not versioned.

### Deviations

- **P0's 0.381 is read from the reference, not from −LN,** as above.
- **Distributed division's gate row is printed but was not registered.** It reads 6/9 on allclose, 0/9 on
  every other arm, and admitted, under both bundles. No registered statistic or verdict depends on it.

### Threats to validity

- **Scaled, offset and constant rows are CPU only.** The device rows reach a width of 129 and an offset of
  about 12.5. Set D shows the bound's headroom on NumPy kernels, and the device's behaviour there is
  inferred from its matching the CPU on the rows it has.
- **Two sm_86 devices, one arithmetic form.** The Triton kernel and its CUDA port share one form, and the
  correct Triton `rsqrt` rewrite is measured only as a NumPy analogue.
- **The law was designed knowing the wrong families.** Its threshold was fixed before its numbers existed
  (it is `DEFAULT_THRESH`), but its slope check and its constant-row reading were written with them in
  view. Set D's inputs and wrong-kernel list were registered before the run.
- **Few distinct correct behaviours:** the 29 CPU kernels show three distinct worst cases on the gate, and
  the loop candidates all reach the stock kernel's ratio.
- **Three significant figures:** the law reports its ratio at that precision, so P0 is checked at that
  precision.
