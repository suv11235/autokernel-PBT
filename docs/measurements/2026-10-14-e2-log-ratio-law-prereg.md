# Pre-registration: E2 for softmax. Does the log-ratio law reject every family the current bundle admits, with no new false positive?

**Date:** 2026-10-14 (registered after the law's spec and before any code that implements or
measures it; results are appended below the line, never edited above it)
**Segment:** generation. This is the first half of the paper's E2 ("complete the bundle"): softmax's
log-ratio law. Layernorm's affine-tie law and the formal half are not part of it.
**Instance:** Apple M4 Pro, CPU only; the project's pinned environment (NumPy 1.26.4)
**Feature:** `specs/features/0014-log-ratio-law/spec.md` (the law's float form, fixed there)
**Companions:** `2026-10-14-log-ratio-headroom-prereg.md` (the statistic's headroom, which this
law is built on), `2026-10-01-softmax-temperature-blind-spot.md` (the saboteur matrix),
`2026-10-14-symmetry-probe.md` on branch `record-symmetry-probe` (two more softmax families)

## 0. The question

Paper §5, E2, success: "every family the proof says the old bundle admits is admitted by the old
gate and rejected by the new one. The correct kernel and the positive control stay as they are,
and no new false positive appears on the 26 loop candidates or the 54 device groups."
Falsified by any new false positive. Whether fast-exp is admitted is decided by a stated
accuracy budget.

For softmax, the new bundle is the current one plus `rows_have_constant_log_ratio`, as feature
0014 specifies it. Its float form, its threshold (c = 30, the project's `DEFAULT_THRESH`) and
its handling of masked and underflowed entries were fixed in the spec before this record. The
stated accuracy budget is that same c = 30, under which fast-exp is admitted (spec, "Why c = 30").

## 1. Construction

**Bundles.** *Current*: `kernels/tasks/softmax/acceptance.yaml`, unchanged (finite, unit interval,
row sums, shift invariance). *Completed*: `kernels/tasks/softmax/acceptance.completed.yaml`, the
same four criteria plus the log-ratio law, last.

**The gate.** `pbt_gate.check_task(kernel, "softmax", bundle=...)`: seed 42, 9 groups,
base + `shift_rows`, four arms. The decisive arm is the declarative one, built from the chosen
bundle. The hybrid arm wraps that same declarative arm. Nothing else in the gate changes.

**Kernel sets.**

| set | kernels | run through |
|---|---|---|
| A. saboteur matrix | the 7 rows of `saboteurs.SABOTEURS`: `beta_1_correct`, `beta_2`, `beta_0`, `beta_minus_1`, `uniform_fill`, `fast_exp`, `unnormalized_positive_control` | the gate, both bundles |
| B. symmetry kernels | softmax with its rows rolled by one, and with its columns reversed: `softmax_reference` with the symmetry applied to its output. They are defined locally, as in `headroom.py`, because `record-symmetry-probe` has not merged | the gate, both bundles |
| C. loop candidates | the 26 iterations of `results.tsv`: the 25 files in `candidates/`, and `best/train.py` (iteration 26, the kept winner, byte-identical to `candidates/r6_best_nolrmult.py`) | the gate, both bundles |
| D. A10 runs | the stock Triton softmax in `runs/gpu-softmax` (9 groups, base + shift), `runs/gpu-tolerance_sweep` (7, base only) and `runs/gpu-scale` (5, base only): 21 groups, 30 executions, read offline | each run's own task contract (*current*), and the same plus the law (*completed*), as declarative arms |
| E. wide and masked rows | correct: the reference, the float32 stock kernel, the `exp2` and reciprocal-multiply analogues (`headroom.py`), and every loop candidate except fast-exp (29 kernels, as in the headroom record). Reported, not classified: fast-exp. Wrong: softmax(2x), softmax(−x), the 1/n fill, rows rolled, columns reversed | the law alone, on inputs below |

**Set E's inputs** are drawn outside the corpus generator, so no recorded or regenerated byte
changes. They come from `np.random.default_rng([20261014, k])`, float32, at two shapes,
(64, 129) and (16, 4096):

- *wide*: x ~ N(0, σ²) for σ ∈ {1, 4, 16, 64}; σ = 64 gives row ranges in the hundreds, far past
  float32's underflow at about 87;
- *masked*: for σ ∈ {1, 16}, each entry is masked with probability 1/4, and one random entry per
  row is kept unmasked. The masked entries are set to −∞ in one variant, and to −1e9 (an
  additive mask, a finite input) in another.

**Measured per case:** the law's verdict and detail, which reports the ratio on a PASS and the
violated check on a FAIL. The gate sets also record each arm's groups failed, the property that
failed first, and the gate's decision.

## 2. Already known, so not predictions

- **The law's statistic on unmasked, non-underflowing data** is the headroom record's
  exploratory spread / (1 + row range). On the gate's cases: correct CPU kernels at most 1.05,
  fast-exp 8.97, every wrong case whose output differs from the reference at least 6.38e6. On
  the A10 executions, at most 1.43. No correct output there underflows.
- **The current bundle's verdicts.** The saboteur matrix as `test_gate_saboteurs.py` pins it. The
  symmetry kernels: admitted, rows rolled caught by 6 of 9 groups against a reference, and columns
  reversed by 7 (probe record). All 26 loop candidates admitted (`results.tsv`). On the A10 runs,
  the declarative arm failed no group (`2026-08-17-first-hardware-run.md`,
  `2026-08-17-at-scale-hardware-run.md`).

## 3. Predictions

- **P0 (implementation).** The law's ratio reproduces the known numbers above to three
  significant figures: 1.05 (CPU correct, maximum), 8.97 (fast-exp), 1.43 (A10, maximum), and
  6.38e6 (wrong, minimum over cases whose output differs).
- **P1 (the current bundle is unchanged).** Every current-bundle cell of sets A–D reproduces the
  known verdicts exactly.
- **P2 (saboteur matrix, completed).** `beta_2`, `beta_0`, `beta_minus_1` and `uniform_fill`:
  declarative 7/9 and hybrid 7/9, both through `rows_have_constant_log_ratio` (the hybrid arm
  short-circuits on its declarative half). Allclose and reference stay at 7/9. The gate
  **rejects** them. `beta_1_correct` and `fast_exp`: 0/9 on every arm, and admitted.
  `unnormalized_positive_control`: unchanged, declarative and hybrid 7/9 through
  `rows_sum_to_one`, and rejected.
- **P3 (the current record breaks exactly where it should).** Read against the completed gate,
  the current record is contradicted in exactly 12 places: the declarative cell, the hybrid cell
  and the admission of each of those four kernels. No other cell of set A changes.
- **P4 (symmetry kernels, completed).** Rows rolled: declarative 6/9 and hybrid 6/9 through the
  law, rejected. Columns reversed: 7/9 and 7/9 through the law, rejected. Allclose and reference
  are unchanged (6 and 7).
- **P5 (loop candidates).** All 26 are admitted under the completed bundle, with the declarative
  arm 0/9 on each and every other arm as under the current one.
- **P6 (A10).** The completed arm fails no group of the 21, and the law is never INCONCLUSIVE on
  any of the 30 executions.
- **P7 (wide rows, correct).** Every correct kernel passes on every wide case, σ = 64 included.
  Their largest ratio is at most **3**: the spec's error model allows about 2 for the `exp2`
  route and about 1 otherwise. Zero or subnormal outputs appear at σ = 16 and σ = 64, and check 4
  rejects none of them.
- **P8 (masked rows, correct).** Every correct kernel outputs exactly 0 at every −∞ entry and
  passes. With −1e9 masks, every correct kernel outputs 0 there and passes, check 4 included.
- **P9 (wide and masked rows, wrong).** Every wrong kernel fails on every set-E case where its
  output is finite and differs from the reference's. One exception is registered now: softmax(−x)
  on −∞ masks turns −∞ into +∞, so its output is NaN. The law leaves that to `outputs_are_finite`
  (INCONCLUSIVE), and it is reported, not counted as a miss.
- **P10 (fast-exp, reported).** Its ratio on set E stays below 30. Its exp error is relative, about
  constant in the argument, so the ratio should fall as the range grows.

**Falsifier (E2's own).** Any correct kernel failing the law on any case of sets A–E: a new false
positive, so this float form is wrong for E2. Also any family the current gate admits that the
completed gate admits on a group where the family's output differs from the reference.

## 4. What is NOT measured

- **Layernorm and its affine-tie law,** and the formal half of E2 (mechanizing the rest of
  Proposition 1). E2 is therefore started here, not completed.
- **Masked or wide inputs through the gate or the corpus.** The gate's domain is N(0, 1) and never
  sends −∞. Set E is offline, and the gate's verdicts on such inputs are not measured.
- **The device on wide or masked rows.** The A10 runs reach a row range of 8.4. On the CPU, set E
  tests the range-aware bound far past that, but whether the A10's 1.6 eps per unit of range
  holds out to a range of 87 is extrapolated.
- **The 54 device groups behind the paper's false-positive figure.** The `exp2` and
  reciprocal-multiply Triton runs are not versioned in `runs/`, so only their NumPy analogues and
  the stock Triton kernel are measured.
- **What a kernel should output on a fully masked row,** and any agent or loop run (E3).

## 5. Deviation rules

- The law, c = 30, the kernel sets and the inputs do not change after the first number is seen.
- A correct case that fails is reported with its detail, never dropped or excused.
- If a kernel cannot be loaded or run on an input, it is reported as such, not replaced.
- The saboteur matrix's completed rows are recorded from what is measured. If a measured cell
  differs from P2–P4, the record says so; the rows are never edited to match a prediction.

### Predictions

- **P0 holds for three of four numbers and fails for fast-exp.** Correct CPU kernels reach at
  most 1.05 over 522 gate cases, the A10 1.43, and the weakest of 82 differing wrong cases
  6.38e6, all as known. Fast-exp reaches **17.2**, not 8.97. The headroom record's 8.97 was the
  per-range value of fast-exp's worst-*spread* case, (7, 129). Its worst *per-range* case is
  (8, 8), at 17.19: a reporting defect in that record. An erratum is appended there. No verdict
  changes, since 17.2 < 30 and fast-exp is still admitted. But its margin under the budget is
  1.7×, not 3.3×.
- **P1 holds.** Every current-bundle cell of sets A–D reproduces the known verdicts.
- **P2 holds** in every cell.
- **P3 holds.** The completed gate contradicts the current record in exactly 12 places: the
  declarative cell, the hybrid cell and the admission of `beta_2`, `beta_0`, `beta_minus_1` and
  `uniform_fill`. Nothing else in set A moves.
- **P4 holds.** Rows rolled: 6/9 and 6/9 through the law, rejected. Columns reversed: 7/9 and 7/9,
  rejected. Allclose and reference stay at 6 and 7.
- **P5 holds.** All 26 loop candidates are admitted, with the declarative arm 0/9 and no other arm
  changed.
- **P6 holds.** No A10 group fails under the completed bundle, and the law is never INCONCLUSIVE
  on an A10 execution.
- **P7 holds.** All correct kernels pass every wide input, σ = 64 included (row ranges in the
  hundreds). The worst ratio is 1.38, under the registered 3. Zeros and subnormals appear at
  σ = 16 and 64, and none is rejected.
- **P8 holds.** Every correct kernel puts exactly 0 on every −∞ entry and passes. With −1e9 masks,
  every correct kernel outputs 0 there and passes.
- **P9 holds.** Every wrong kernel fails every set-E case where its output is finite and differs,
  76 of 76. The registered exception: softmax(−x) on −∞ masks gives NaN, so the law abstains (4).
- **P10 fails.** Fast-exp fails 8 of the 16 set-E inputs. Wide rows fail at σ = 16, (64, 129), and
  σ = 64 at both shapes, with spread ratios 2.0e6 to 7.0e6. All four −∞ inputs fail the masked
  check. The −1e9 input at σ = 16, (64, 129), fails the spread check. Where it passes, its worst
  ratio is 9.71.
- **E2's falsifier does not fire.** No correct kernel failed the law on any case: 522 gate cases,
  the 26 candidates' 468, 30 A10 executions, and 464 wide and masked cases. No family the current
  gate admits passed the completed gate on a group where its output differs.

### Why fast-exp fails on wide and masked rows (post hoc diagnostic, not in `e2.py`)

`fast_exp` floors its argument at −126 in base 2, which is −87.3 in natural units. Wherever a true
exp underflows, it returns exactly 2⁻¹²⁶ = 1.1754944e-38, the smallest *normal* float32. When a
row's sum is close to 1, that value survives the division as a normal output where softmax is,
for example, e^−115. The law judges every normal output, and log y − x there is off by up to the
row's range: on wide σ = 16, (64, 129), 20 such entries, and on σ = 64, (16, 4096), 32,552. On
−∞ masks it leaves about 1e-39 instead of 0 (1.4e-39 to 2.0e-39 on the first −∞ input), which its own docstring states ("causal to
within 1e-38 rather than exactly"). The reference arm's budget is normwise and cannot see an
absolute error of 1e-38. The law's budget is componentwise and relative on every normal output,
plus exactly zero on a masked score, and it can.

### What it says for E2

1. **For softmax, the completed bundle meets E2's criterion on everything we hold.** Every family
   Proposition 1(a) admits and the current gate admits is rejected by the completed gate:
   softmax(βx) at β = 2, 0 and −1, the 1/n fill, rows rolled and columns reversed. The correct
   kernel and the positive control are unchanged. No new false positive appears on the 26 loop
   candidates or on the 21 recorded A10 groups. The paper's 54 device groups are only partly
   answered, as registered: their `exp2` and reciprocal-multiply Triton runs are not versioned.
2. **The range-aware bound holds far past every recorded range.** At row ranges in the hundreds,
   with underflow and masking, correct kernels reach 1.38 against a budget of 30, a 22× margin.
   The headroom record's worry, that a flat threshold runs out near a range of 20, does not apply
   to this form.
3. **The accuracy budget admits fast-exp only on the gate's domain.** At c = 30 it is admitted on
   the gate's N(0, 1) cases (17.2), but rejected on rows that underflow or carry −∞ masks, which
   are the inputs the model's real call site sends. E2's own text asks the gate to "cover the
   inputs the real call site sends, including −∞ masked scores". Once the gate's domain does that,
   fast-exp as written is rejected by the completed gate. Whether that is the intended accuracy
   budget is the team's decision. *Hypothesis, untested:* a fast-exp that returned 0 below its
   floor would pass.
4. **E2 is started, not finished.** Layernorm's affine-tie law, the formal half, and masked or wide
   inputs in the gate's own domain remain.

### Deviations

- **Set D's table gained a column after the first run.** The first run printed only the completed
  bundle's INCONCLUSIVE count (5 on `gpu-scale`), which needed the current bundle's count beside
  it to read. No statistic or verdict changed, and the two runs' output is otherwise
  byte-identical.
- **The fast-exp diagnostic above is post hoc,** run ad hoc after the numbers were seen. It is not
  part of `e2.py`.
- **The spec and two code comments cited fast-exp's ratio as 8.97,** taken from the headroom
  record. They now cite 17.2, with the reason. c = 30 and every rule of the law are unchanged.

### Threats to validity

- **Wide and masked rows are CPU only.** The A10 rows reach a range of 8.4. Set E shows the bound's
  headroom on NumPy kernels, and the device's behaviour at wide ranges is extrapolated from it.
- **One device, one compiler, one Triton kernel,** as in the headroom record.
- **The law was designed knowing the wrong families.** Its threshold was fixed before its numbers
  existed (it is `DEFAULT_THRESH`), but its masking and underflow rules were written with them in
  view. Set E's inputs and wrong-kernel list were registered before the run.
- **Few distinct correct behaviours:** on every set-E input the worst correct kernel is the `exp2`
  analogue, and the loop candidates all reach the stock kernel's ratio.
- **Three significant figures:** the law reports its ratio at that precision, so P0 is checked at
  that precision.
